## Surprises / oddities encountered

- The English index has 829 head entries; the French glossary has 927 — French is broader. Many French heads have no English counterpart (e.g. several Latin-tagged variants, narrow sub-headed entries like "Aveu des fautes"). That asymmetry is normal and not the subject of this report.
- 83 English heads are pure cross-references (`X. See Y`). Most resolve cleanly to a French head and were classified as duplicates. Five of them survived as non-duplicates: `Orbit. → Creation; Nature`, `Self-denial. → Ascesis`, `Incredulity. → Unbelief` are functionally duplicates (their targets exist in FR) — flagged in the table so you can fast-skip them. `Popular.` and `Universal.` are meta-references (`See nouns to which it refers`); these are not real concept entries and should be skipped entirely.
- Several English entries end with a stray period (e.g. `Absolution.`, `Bible.`, `Hodie.`). These come straight from the source markup, where every cross-reference head is dot-suffixed; the French glossary has the same artefact in some entries (e.g. `Accusation.`, `Diocèse. définition`, `Alliance ancienne ( foedus vetus ).`).
- A few "non-duplicates" are debatable: `Cruel practices` overlaps with FR `Tortures`; `Funerals` overlaps with `Sépulture`; `Faults` overlaps with `Imputabilité` and `Aveu des fautes`; `Atonement` overlaps with `Réparation` / `Propitiation`. They are listed as non-duplicates because no head match exists, but you may decide they are already covered.
- The French glossary uses `seeAlso` strings (not slugs), which made matching cleaner.
